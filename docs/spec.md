## Problem Statement

The BiteLens owner wants to record meals quickly by taking a photo on an iPhone,
without navigating menus, typing a food diary, or weighing every ingredient.
The owner needs useful, editable estimates of food contents, consumed amounts,
and nutrients, together with a durable food record alongside existing Apple
Health and other health data in Supabase.

The workflow must work around unreliable connectivity and uncertain AI estimates.
Meal Photos must not become a permanent cloud photo archive or appear in the
Photos library. AI provider choice must remain flexible between OpenAI and Grok,
without changing how meals are reviewed, logged, or displayed.

## Solution

Build a personal, native iPhone app for iOS 18 or newer. After one-time email
sign-in and provider selection, BiteLens opens directly to the camera and also
offers a Lock Screen/Control Center action that opens it after device unlock.
Capture one Meal Photo, optionally add context, and receive a Meal Estimate from
the explicitly selected AI provider through an authenticated Supabase Edge
Function. Review and correct Meal Items, Consumed Portions, and nutrient values,
then confirm the result as a Logged Meal.

Keep Meal Drafts and unconfirmed estimates locally. Confirmed meals appear in
local history and daily totals immediately, including when offline, and their
structured data syncs into dedicated tables in the configured
Supabase project when BiteLens is open and online. Preserve structured history
until the owner deletes it.

Keep Meal Image copies in private app storage for 168 hours from the camera
shutter or image import. Keep copies out of Photos and Supabase Storage. At
expiry, prevent further access and delete the copies when the app next gets
execution time. Cloud AI processing remains
permitted under the accepted standard provider retention policies, which are
distinct from this local photo-history policy.

## Implementation Decisions

1. **Platform.** Build a native SwiftUI iPhone app targeting iOS 18 or newer, using Apple's camera APIs and a system launch action. Require unlock before capture in v1.
2. **Onboarding and identity.** One-time setup signs into the existing email-based Supabase account and selects the initial provider. Persist the session and selection. Reuse the project's existing email authentication flow, verifying its delivery and redirect configuration during setup. BiteLens has no public signup flow. Keep the shared project's signup configuration unchanged.
3. **Feature boundary.** Compose capture, local persistence, analysis, correction, confirmation, history, aggregation, photo expiry, and sync into one meal workflow. Keep a Meal Draft distinct from a Logged Meal; only explicit confirmation makes the meal contribute to logged history and daily totals.
4. **Capture and context.** Start a local Meal Draft with one image from the camera, Photos, or the iOS Share Sheet. When the source app offers a document handoff, choosing BiteLens opens the app and creates the draft for review. **Save to BiteLens** saves the shared image and requests that iOS open BiteLens to the draft. If iOS declines the switch, the draft opens the next time BiteLens becomes active. Camera captures support retakes before analysis. Imported images can be food photos or screenshots of itemized receipts. Tap the draft preview to see the image full screen and pinch to zoom. Allow optional ingredient and serving notes, and keep analysis and confirmation explicit. Disclose that the selected AI provider receives the image and any visible receipt details.
5. **Local persistence.** Keep private photo files separate from structured local data. Persist drafts, unconfirmed estimates, confirmed local meals, and pending sync operations durably. Unconfirmed structured estimates remain on the device; the backend returns analysis results without persisting those drafts.
6. **Meal Image lifetime.** Keep app-managed copies in private app storage with device protection and requested backup exclusion. Keep image bytes out of Photos writes, cloud storage, structured database fields, sync payloads, and app logs. Expire access 168 hours after the camera shutter, Photos import, or Share Sheet save. Edits, retries, and confirmation leave the deadline unchanged. Delete expired copies when the app next gets execution time.
7. **Expired drafts.** Apply image expiry to every draft, including one never analyzed. Once its image is unavailable, offer manual entry or discard. Retain any existing textual estimate independently of the image lifetime.
8. **Server-side analysis contract.** An authenticated Supabase Edge Function accepts the selected provider, photo bytes, and optional meal context. It checks the approved owner's identity before provider work and returns a validated, provider-independent Meal Estimate. The common result describes Meal Items, estimated grams, serving descriptions, optional Consumed Counts, the tracked nullable nutrient values, and provider/model provenance. Schema validation does not establish nutritional accuracy; review remains required.
9. **Provider adapters and configuration.** Support OpenAI and Grok behind the same interface. Keep credentials and per-provider model configuration server-side. Use supported image-input and structured-output capabilities, validating provider-specific responses before normalization. Transfer image bytes for analysis without creating an app-managed cloud photo archive. Disable saved response history. Select and verify compatible models during implementation rather than hardcoding an unverified model choice into this spec.
10. **Provider selection and failure recovery.** Selection is explicit through Settings or a retry action and applies to new analyses. Never fall back automatically to the other provider or rewrite historical meals because selection changed. Failed or timed-out analysis preserves the draft and existing edits, offering retry, an explicitly chosen alternative provider, or manual entry.
11. **Accepted provider retention.** The owner accepted standard OpenAI API retention, including potential content retention for up to 30 days subject to documented exceptions, with saved responses disabled. The owner also accepted standard Grok API retention, normally 30 days for abuse auditing subject to documented exceptions, with saved response history disabled. Grok Zero Data Retention is optional. These policies are distinct from the app's seven-day photo history and do not imply that disabling response history eliminates all provider retention.
12. **Estimate and portion semantics.** AI directly estimates nutrients in v1. A Consumed Portion represents what was actually eaten. Use estimated grams, familiar serving descriptions, and an optional positive Consumed Count for interchangeable pieces. Counts can be fractional. Weight and nutrients describe the whole item. Group pieces within a meal and keep separately editable foods as separate items. Preserve existing items during migration. Changing one known count to another scales known weight and nutrients by the new count divided by the previous count. Adding the first count preserves existing amounts. Changing consumed weight scales known nutrients. Direct weight and nutrient edits remain available, and unknown values stay unknown. Analysis distinguishes food photos from itemized receipts. Show identifiable receipt items and any legible Purchased Quantity, which starts as an editable Consumed Count. Keep the purchased quantity in the draft and save the confirmed portion in the meal record.
13. **Nutrients and aggregation.** Track calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sodium, alcohol, caffeine, and added sugar per Meal Item and in meal and daily totals. Display calories in kcal, sodium and caffeine in milligrams, and other tracked nutrients in grams. Alcohol is grams of pure alcohol. Show the new amounts in item review, meal details, and daily totals. Keep history rows compact. Missing values, including newly tracked values for older records, remain unknown, distinct from measured or estimated zero. Allow confirmation of Incomplete Estimates and visibly mark affected totals incomplete.
14. **Meal timing.** Default camera drafts to shutter time. For an imported image, suggest the receipt order time, the receipt email's header time when there is no order time, or a food photo's original timestamp. When analysis provides no usable time, read a clear email header time on the device. If only a time is visible, use the date of import and label the assumption in the draft. Honor a printed UTC offset when available and show the device time zone used as the default. Preserve manually entered Meal Time and require a choice before confirmation when there is no usable suggestion. Retain the meal's local date and time zone for daily grouping, including travel. Keep the image retention clock independent of Meal Time.
15. **Structured cloud data.** Use dedicated meal and Meal Item tables in the configured Supabase project. Records need stable client-generated identities, the configured Supabase owner UUID, meal timing and local date and time zone, descriptions, portions, nullable nutrients, notes, and estimate provenance as appropriate. Retain the Original Item Estimate for AI-derived items in a Logged Meal. Distinguish AI-derived items with current values that match or differ from the original, manually added items, and items whose provenance is unknown. Compare the current and original values across edits before and after confirmation. Restoring the original values clears the correction marker. Store the original and current versions for comparison. Structured records outlive photo expiry and persist until explicitly deleted.
16. **Shared-project authorization.** Follow the existing ownership convention based on a required user UUID referencing Supabase Auth. Enable owner-specific RLS and explicit operation grants on new client-facing meal tables. Enforce the approved BiteLens owner in both data access and analysis authorization; authentication alone does not authorize every project user. Do not rely on client-editable metadata for authorization, expose privileged keys to the app, alter existing health tables, or change shared signup settings.
17. **Offline confirmation and synchronization.** Permit confirming an already analyzed meal offline. Apply confirmed creates, edits, and deletions to local history and totals immediately, with pending sync status. Retry structured sync when BiteLens is open and online. Use stable identities and reconciliation to keep one consistent meal snapshot across repeated or interrupted requests. Keep pending changes durable across app restarts. Require server timestamps with current-time defaults. The first successful save sets `created_at`, and each accepted new revision, including an item-only edit, advances `updated_at`. Preserve both on an identical retry. Fill missing legacy values from `confirmed_at`, preserving recorded server times. Privileged SQL readers can detect new deletions by the first accepted deletion's `deleted_at`. Identical retries preserve it, and legacy deletion times remain unknown.
18. **History and deletion.** Provide meal history, detail review, and daily totals. Edits and deletions update the affected totals. A local photo is available only while its retention window permits; structured history remains after it expires. Propagate offline deletions through the same pending sync workflow.
    - Start History in Day and remember the last view for the BiteLens Account. Week, Month, and All time summarize one tracked nutrient at a time. Start with protein and retain the nutrient selection across summaries.
    - Use recorded local dates and include days through yesterday. Count a day for the selected nutrient if it has at least one Logged Meal and every Meal Item has a value for that nutrient. A recorded zero counts.
    - Show Monday-to-Sunday weeks and calendar months newest first. Skip periods with no Logged Meals in the date range. Explain when meals exist but no days qualify for the nutrient, and show the cutoff for an in-progress period.
    - Show the daily average, high, low, and number of days counted. Link each high or low to that date's nutrient totals, meals, and observations. For ties, link the most recent date and show how many days tied.
19. **Targets and delivery.** Use $5/month combined provider usage and 10-20 seconds per analysis as initial measurement targets, with immediate local capture. They are not claims of verified cost or performance. Plan ongoing personal installation through Xcode with a paid Apple Developer account. Verify the actual device, Xcode/iOS SDK, signing, provider credentials, and email delivery during implementation setup.

## Apple Health Sync

- **Opt in from Settings.** Automatically sync meals first confirmed while Apple Health Sync is on. Eligibility follows confirmation, so a meal confirmed today with yesterday's Meal Time can still sync.
- **Share meal totals.** Write one quantity sample per complete, authorized nutrient for each eligible Logged Meal, using the current values and Meal Time. Share calories (kcal), protein, carbohydrates, total fat, and fiber (g), and sodium and caffeine (mg). A known zero is eligible. If any Meal Item has an unknown amount for a nutrient, omit that nutrient's total. Food names, item breakdowns, images, added sugar, and grams of alcohol stay in BiteLens.
- **Share alcohol intake.** Convert a complete, positive meal alcohol total from grams of pure alcohol to fractional U.S. standard drinks by dividing by 14. Write the result at Meal Time to Apple Health's alcoholic beverage count when authorized. Skip zero and unknown totals. Remove BiteLens's alcohol sample if a correction takes the total to zero. [HealthKit alcoholic beverage type](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/numberofalcoholicbeverages) · [NIAAA standard drink definition](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/alcohols-effects-health/what-standard-drink)
- **Backfill from Settings.** With Apple Health Sync on, **Sync Last 30 Days** selects Logged Meals in the iPhone's local BiteLens history, including meals with pending Supabase uploads. Use the recorded local Meal Time to select today and the previous 29 calendar days. Require write access for at least one supported data type before enrolling the meals. If all are blocked, show that access is needed so the owner can grant it and start again. Resume interrupted work for the same meals using their latest revisions. Selected meals stay eligible for updates and deletions after they age out of the window. See [ADR 0006](adr/0006-keep-backfilled-meals-in-apple-health-sync.md).
- **Reflect meal changes.** Update BiteLens's samples when their totals or Meal Time change. Remove a sample when its total becomes incomplete, and remove the meal's samples when the meal is deleted. A later correction can bring back a sample that BiteLens removed because a total was incomplete or alcohol was zero. If the owner deletes a BiteLens sample in Apple Health, keep that entry absent during automatic sync and subsequent backfills. The Logged Meal remains in BiteLens.
- **Pause and resume.** Turning sync off pauses writes and preserves existing Health entries. Turning it back on resumes work for previously eligible meals, including pending edits and deletions. Meals first confirmed while sync is off can be included through **Sync Last 30 Days**. When a nutrient's write permission is granted or restored, catch up for eligible meals under the same rules.
- **Save meals independently.** Request write access for the supported nutrients and sync whichever ones are authorized. HealthKit failures never block meal confirmation, edits, or deletion. Keep pending work across app restarts and retry temporary failures when the app next opens. Settings shows which nutrients are authorized, surfaces pending or failed writes, and offers a retry. See [ADR 0005](adr/0005-export-nutrient-totals-from-the-iphone.md).
- **Connect one account in Beta.** The first confirmed meal or symptom opt-in connects the active BiteLens Account to Apple Health for that installation. Meal sync and symptom sharing have separate toggles. The connected account can export while signed in and using its local record. Signing out or switching accounts pauses its work and preserves existing samples. See [ADR 0010](adr/0010-connect-one-beta-account-to-apple-health.md).

## Testing Decisions

- **Primary seam:** test the composed meal workflow through its public actions and observable results, from Meal Draft to reviewed Meal Estimate to Logged Meal, including history/totals, persistence, expiry, and sync. Prefer this single feature-level integration boundary over separate seams for each view model, helper, or provider SDK wrapper.
- **Controlled dependencies:** use a controllable clock and deterministic provider responses at the network boundary. Exercise actual local persistence and temporary private photo files, including reopening the workflow to observe restored state. Use an isolated Supabase test environment for real authorization and persistence checks; never alter production health data for tests.
- **Backend boundary checks:** exercise the public authenticated analysis/data interfaces for both providers, including unauthorized requests and invalid ownership. These checks complement the primary workflow suite where backend behavior cannot be established by a simulated mobile transport alone.
- **Device checks:** use a small real-device smoke suite for camera permissions, capture, retake, launch after unlock, and the Lock Screen/Control Center action. Avoid trying to prove camera/system integration solely through unit mocks.
- **What makes a good test:** assert externally observable behavior and persisted outcomes. Check that the intended user action produces the correct record, total, access decision, provider request, or unavailable photo. Do not assert private method calls, class layout, SDK internals, or a prescribed implementation structure.
- **Capture and drafts:** offline capture saves immediately; a draft survives restart; optional notes remain optional; retakes replace the selected photo; unconfirmed estimates do not enter Supabase or logged totals.
- **Provider contract:** run shared fixtures through both adapters and verify normalized item descriptions, portions, nullable nutrients, units, and provenance. Verify schema-invalid, incomplete, refused, timed-out, and failed responses produce recoverable states without destroying drafts or edits. Selection must control the provider receiving the next request, with no cross-provider fallback or historical rewrites.
- **Corrections and totals.** Use a grouped estimate of five spring rolls with 250 g and 400 kcal. Changing the count to three yields 150 g and 240 kcal and shows a correction. Restoring the original values clears the marker. Test fractional counts, recording a first count, and direct nutrient edits. Verify unknown values stay unknown, known zero stays valid, and incomplete meal and daily totals are visible.
- **Confirmation and sync.** Verify offline confirmation updates local history and totals immediately, pending work survives restart, and retries converge on one consistent meal snapshot. A meal confirmed offline keeps that time when uploaded. Its first successful save sets server timestamps, an accepted item edit advances `updated_at`, and an identical retry preserves both timestamps. Verify legacy records and queued uploads can still be read and synced.
- **Apple Health Sync.** Verify opt-in eligibility, permission changes, pause and resume, incomplete totals, time and portion edits, deletions, and recovery across app restarts through the meal workflow. Verify that **Sync Last 30 Days** selects local meals by recorded local Meal Time, including pending Supabase uploads, and that backfilled meals stay eligible after they age out of the window. Verify that 21 g of pure alcohol yields 1.5 standard drinks, a zero or unknown total produces no sample, and a correction to zero removes an existing sample. On an iPhone, verify that authorized samples appear once at Meal Time, update after corrections, and stay absent if manually removed in Health, including after another backfill. Verify Health unavailability leaves local meal logging usable.
- **Time and retention:** advance the clock across the 168-hour boundary and verify expired photo access and analysis are denied, cleanup occurs when execution resumes, and edited meal time or retries never extend photo lifetime. Cover unanalyzed drafts, textual estimates after expiry, delayed confirmation, local-day boundaries, and travel/time-zone changes.
- **Privacy and authorization:** verify no Photos-library writes, Supabase photo uploads, photo-bearing structured sync payloads, or raw photo/credential logs. Check file protection and backup-exclusion configuration through observable file metadata. Verify unsigned and unapproved users cannot analyze, read, insert, edit, delete, or reassign ownership of meal records; rejected analysis requests must not reach either provider.
- **Live evaluation:** after secure credential setup, use a small representative meal-photo set with both providers to measure latency, usage cost, estimate usefulness, and correction effort. Treat outputs as approximate and human-reviewed; do not encode unverifiable exact nutrient ground truth as a model test. Routine automated tests should not require billable live inference.

## Out of Scope

- Multiple BiteLens users, public signup, sharing, and social features.
- Importing Apple Health data into BiteLens and combined health-data dashboards.
- Changes to existing health tables or shared project signup settings.
- Capturing while the device remains locked through a dedicated camera extension.
- Multiple images per meal or importing a message directly from email.
- Permanent photo history, Photos-library saving, Supabase photo storage, or an app-managed cloud image archive.
- Cloud synchronization of Meal Drafts or unconfirmed estimates.
- Automatic cross-provider fallback, automatic confirmation, or rewriting historical meals after a provider switch.
- A mandatory Zero Data Retention prerequisite for Grok.
- A separate food-composition database, exhaustive micronutrients, or guaranteed measured nutrition accuracy.
- Cross-meal food rankings, saturated fat and potassium tracking, model confidence scores, and mirroring meal deletions into downstream systems beyond Apple Health Sync.
- Exact background physical deletion when iOS does not execute the app, or an absolute device-backup exclusion guarantee.
- Public App Store or TestFlight distribution for v1.
- Treating the budget/latency targets as already verified results or adding an unrequested enforced billing cap.

## Further Notes

- The owner explicitly confirmed the v1 design after the design interview. This specification synthesizes those decisions; it does not reopen the interview.
- Preserve the glossary meanings of Meal, Meal Photo, Meal Item, Meal Estimate, Incomplete Estimate, Meal Draft, Consumed Portion, and Logged Meal. Respect the accepted local-photo-retention and server-side-provider-interface ADRs.
- Before BiteLens, schema inspection found HealthKit metrics, daily summaries, and user settings using an owner UUID linked to Supabase Auth. All six public tables inspected had RLS enabled. BiteLens added dedicated meal tables. Existing identity provider labels include only email; that does not identify the current password/link/code login method.
- HealthKit event timestamps use time-zone-aware timestamps; daily summaries use a date, and user settings have a time-zone field. Meal grouping follows the recorded local date/time zone chosen in this design.
- See [iPhone setup](ios-setup.md) for signing and [meal analysis](testing/meal-analysis.md) for provider configuration.
- Standard provider retention was accepted separately from local photo storage. Retention controls and compatible model capabilities must be checked against current official documentation when implementing: [OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data), [xAI security guidance](https://docs.x.ai/developers/faq/security), [Apple backup guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/optimizing-your-app-s-data-for-icloud-backup), and [Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security).
